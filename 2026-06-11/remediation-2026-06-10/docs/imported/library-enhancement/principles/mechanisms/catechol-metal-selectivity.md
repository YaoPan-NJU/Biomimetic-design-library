# Catechol Coordination Selectivity for Borderline-Soft Metals

> Rule ID: CM-014 | Confidence: 0.85 | Last validated: 2026-06-05 (LLM draft, pending literature validation)

## Core Claim

Catechol, as a hard O-donor ligand, preferentially coordinates with hard and borderline metal ions, with highest practical selectivity for Fe3+, Pb2+, and Cu2+ among common heavy metal pollutants.

## Detailed Explanation

Catechol provides two hard oxygen donor atoms in a rigid bidentate arrangement. Per HSAB theory, hard O-donors prefer hard and borderline metal ions. The selectivity order reflects a combination of ionic charge, ionic radius, and crystal field effects:

**Selectivity order for catechol-metal coordination:**

Fe3+ >> Cu2+ > Pb2+ > Ni2+ > Zn2+ > Cd2+ > Mn2+

**Quantitative stability constants (log beta for bis-catecholate complexes):**

| Metal | log beta2 | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Fe3+ (tris) | ~35-40 | Exceptional: 3+ charge + optimal radius + high CFSE |
| Cu2+ | ~18-22 | Jahn-Teller distortion enhances equatorial bonding |
| Pb2+ | ~14-18 | Large borderline ion, good match for O-donor cavity |
| Ni2+ | ~12-16 | High CFSE (d8) |
| Zn2+ | ~10-12 | d10, no CFSE |
| Cd2+ | ~8-10 | Larger, softer than Zn2+ |
| Mn2+ | ~6-8 | d5, zero CFSE |

**Biological significance:**

The exceptional Fe3+-catechol affinity is exploited by marine organisms. Siderophores (iron-chelating molecules) produced by marine bacteria often contain catechol groups precisely because of the extremely high Fe3+ selectivity (log beta > 35 for tris-catecholate complexes). Mussel foot proteins similarly benefit from catechol-Fe3+ coordination for byssus thread crosslinking.

**Practical implications for water treatment:**

1. **Pb2+ removal**: Catechol-based adsorbents show high selectivity for Pb2+ over most other divalent metals. In Pb2+/Zn2+ mixtures, selectivity ratios of 10-50:1 are achievable.

2. **Cu2+ removal**: Excellent Cu2+ selectivity, though Fe3+ (if present) will compete strongly.

3. **Cd2+ removal**: Relatively poor selectivity for Cd2+ via catechol alone. Thiol-functional groups are far superior for Cd2+ selective removal.

4. **Interference from Fe3+**: In iron-rich water (e.g., acid mine drainage), Fe3+ will saturate catechol sites before other metals, potentially reducing capacity for target pollutants.

## Positive Example

Mussel-inspired adsorbents with high DOPA density achieve Pb2+ selectivity factors of 10-20x over Zn2+ in equimolar mixtures at pH 6. The selectivity arises from Pb2+'s optimal match with the catechol O-donor cavity size (Pb2+ radius 1.19 A fits well in the 5-membered chelate ring).

## Counter-Example / Boundary Condition

At very high pH (>10), the selectivity order may shift because metal hydroxide precipitation becomes competitive with coordination. Fe3+ precipitates as Fe(OH)3 at pH > 3, so in practice Fe3+ interference is only relevant at very low pH. For Cd2+ selective removal, catechol-based adsorbents are not recommended; thiol-based alternatives are preferred.

## Applicable Prototypes

- **mussel-foot-adhesion**: DOPA-catechol provides inherent Fe3+ and Pb2+ selectivity; natural function includes Fe3+-mediated crosslinking
- **polydopamine-coating**: PDA residual catechol groups show similar selectivity trends
- **plant-tannin**: Multiple catechol-type groups enhance overall selectivity for Fe3+ and Pb2+
