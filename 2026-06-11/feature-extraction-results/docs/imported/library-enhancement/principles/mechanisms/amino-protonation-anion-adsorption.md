# Amino Group Protonation Enables Anion Adsorption at Acidic pH

> Rule ID: CM-004 | Confidence: 0.9 | Last validated: 2026-06-05 (LLM draft, pending literature validation)

## Core Claim

Below pH ~6.5, amino groups protonate to -NH3+, switching their function from metal-coordinating ligands to positively charged electrostatic adsorption sites for anionic pollutants.

## Detailed Explanation

The amino group (-NH2) is a weak base with a pKa that depends on its chemical environment:

- Free aliphatic amines: pKa ~ 10.5-11
- Chitosan C2-amino group: pKa ~ 6.3-6.5 (lowered by adjacent electronegative groups in the glucosamine ring)
- Aromatic amines: pKa ~ 4-5

For chitosan and similar biopolymers, the relevant pKa is approximately 6.3-6.5. The protonation equilibrium is:

R-NH2 + H+ <-> R-NH3+ (Ka ~ 10^-6.5)

Below this pKa:

1. **Charge development**: At pH 4, approximately 99% of amino groups are protonated (-NH3+), creating a positively charged surface with zeta potential of +30 to +60 mV.

2. **Electrostatic anion adsorption**: The positive surface charge enables strong electrostatic attraction of anionic species including Cr2O7^2-, HCrO4^-, AsO4^3-, methyl orange (anionic dye), and other negatively charged pollutants.

3. **Loss of coordination ability**: The protonated nitrogen has no available lone pair for dative bonding to metal cations. Cu2+, Zn2+, Ni2+, and other transition metals that would normally coordinate with -NH2 cannot bind to -NH3+.

4. **Dual functionality switch**: This creates a remarkable pH-switchable behavior -- the same amino group adsorbs anions at low pH (electrostatic mechanism) and coordinates metals at higher pH (coordination mechanism). The transition occurs over approximately 2 pH units centered on the pKa.

The degree of deacetylation (DDA) of chitosan directly determines the density of amino groups and therefore the magnitude of this effect. Fully deacetylated chitosan (DDA > 95%) shows the strongest pH-dependent switching.

## Positive Example

Chitosan achieves Cr(VI) adsorption capacities of 80-200 mg/g at pH 2-4, where Cr(VI) exists primarily as HCrO4^- and amino groups are fully protonated. The adsorption mechanism is electrostatic attraction between R-NH3+ and HCrO4^-. At pH 7, Cr(VI) adsorption drops to <10 mg/g as amino groups deprotonate and Cr(VI) exists as CrO4^2-.

## Counter-Example / Boundary Condition

Crosslinked chitosan (e.g., glutaraldehyde-crosslinked) has some amino groups consumed in Schiff base formation (R-N=CH-), reducing the available amino density and the magnitude of the pH-switching effect. Highly crosslinked chitosan may show only moderate pH dependence.

## Applicable Prototypes

- **chitosan**: The primary amino group at C2 position provides the pH-switchable behavior; this is the defining characteristic of chitosan as an adsorbent
- **chlorella-cell-wall**: Cell wall proteins contain amino groups (from lysine, arginine residues) that undergo similar protonation, though with different pKa values
- **mycelium**: Fungal cell wall chitosan and protein amino groups protonate at acidic pH
- **scallop-shell**: Organic matrix proteins in nacre contain amino groups with pH-dependent behavior
