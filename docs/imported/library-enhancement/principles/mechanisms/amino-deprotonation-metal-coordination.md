# Amino Group Deproteination Enables Metal Coordination

> Rule ID: CM-005 | Confidence: 0.88 | Last validated: 2026-06-05 (LLM draft, pending literature validation)

## Core Claim

At pH 5-9, amino groups progressively deprotonate, freeing the nitrogen lone pair for coordination with transition metal ions, with maximum coordination near pH 7-8.

## Detailed Explanation

As pH increases above the amino group's pKa (~6.3-6.5 for chitosan), the equilibrium shifts from the protonated ammonium form (-NH3+) to the free amine form (-NH2):

R-NH3+ <-> R-NH2 + H+

The free amine nitrogen possesses a lone pair of electrons in an sp3 hybrid orbital, which can be donated to empty metal d-orbitals to form a coordinate covalent bond (dative bond):

R-NH2 + M^n+ -> [R-NH2 -> M]^(n+)

Key aspects of amino-metal coordination:

1. **pH-dependent fraction**: The fraction of free -NH2 groups follows the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:
   - At pH = pKa (6.5): 50% -NH2, 50% -NH3+
   - At pH 7.5: ~90% -NH2
   - At pH 8.5: ~99% -NH2

2. **Coordination geometry**: Amino groups typically form tetrahedral or square planar complexes with transition metals. Chitosan's C2-amino and C3-hydroxyl groups can act as a bidentate ligand, forming a stable 5-membered chelate ring.

3. **Metal preference**: Per HSAB theory, -NH2 is a borderline Lewis base, preferentially coordinating with borderline Lewis acids: Cu2+ > Ni2+ > Co2+ > Zn2+ > Cd2+ > Mn2+ (Irving-Williams series).

4. **Upper pH limit**: Above pH ~9, dissolved metal ions begin forming insoluble hydroxide precipitates (e.g., Cu(OH)2, Ksp ~ 2.2 x 10^-20). Precipitation competes with coordination, and the adsorption measurement becomes confounded by co-precipitation.

5. **Chelate enhancement**: When multiple amino groups are in close proximity (e.g., chitosan chain with repeating glucosamine units), multidentate coordination is possible, significantly enhancing complex stability via the chelate effect.

## Positive Example

Chitosan achieves Cu2+ adsorption capacities of 60-180 mg/g at pH 5-7, with adsorption increasing as pH rises from 5 to 7. The Cu2+ is coordinated by 2-4 amino groups from adjacent glucosamine units. XPS analysis confirms Cu-N bonding with N 1s peak shift from 399.3 eV (free -NH2) to 399.8 eV (coordinated -NH2->Cu).

## Counter-Example / Boundary Condition

At high chitosan concentrations or in crosslinked chitosan films, amino groups may be sterically inaccessible even when deprotonated. Dense polymer packing limits metal ion diffusion to interior coordination sites. Swelling the polymer (e.g., by pH adjustment or solvent exchange) can partially overcome this limitation.

## Applicable Prototypes

- **chitosan**: Primary amino group at C2 position; C3-OH and C6-OH provide auxiliary coordination, creating a tridentate binding pocket
- **chlorella-cell-wall**: Protein amino groups (lysine epsilon-NH2, arginine guanidino group) deprotonate at different pH values, providing a broader coordination pH range
- **mycelium**: Fungal chitosan and cell wall protein amino groups
- **scallop-shell**: Nacre organic matrix proteins contain lysine and histidine residues with pH-dependent coordination
